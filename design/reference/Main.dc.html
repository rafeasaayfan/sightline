Players don't watch a film. They watch a match.

Every video player is designed for a story that goes one way. Gameplay has angles, rounds and moments you want to jump between. Sightline is a Video.js v10 skin built for that.

One stage, two synced angles you can swap with a click or the keys 1–3, and a timeline where every elimination, objective and round is a marker you can seek to. Everything works by keyboard, touch and mouse.

Angles are real controls

The angle chips swap the source on the stage and seek to the same timestamp. Tiles stay muted and resync every 2 seconds.

A timeline of moments

Eliminations, objectives and rounds come from a WebVTT chapter file. N jumps to the next highlight; markers have tooltips and are keyboard-reachable.

Keyboard like a spectator client

1–3 angles, N next highlight, Space, M, F, arrows. Focus is always visible and the band never hides while something inside it has focus.

Readable over anything

Controls sit on a gradient scrim, never on raw footage. Tested over a snow map and a night map: 4.5:1 or better everywhere.

Phone first

On a phone the angles become a chip row under the stage and tap targets grow to 48 px. Swipe the recap to jump between moments.

Calm on request

Under reduced motion the event toast and sync pulse go still and swaps cut instead of fade. Under forced colours the markers keep their shapes.
